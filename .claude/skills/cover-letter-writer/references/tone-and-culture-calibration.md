# Calibrating tone by company and culture

## The core EN/DE contrast

English-language letters, especially to startups and most tech companies,
can carry overt confidence and directness without reading badly: "I've
spent three years building exactly this" is a normal, well-received line.

German defaults to sachlich (factual/measured) and expects a degree of
Bescheidenheit (modesty). The same overt confidence translated word-for-
word into German reads as unseriös, not impressive. The way personality
comes through in a German letter is via specific, checkable factual claims
— not adjectives, not superlatives, not "Ich bin die ideale Besetzung für
diese Stelle." State the fact and let the reader draw that conclusion.

This means: drafting the German and English versions of a letter for the
same posting is not a translation exercise — the register itself shifts,
not just the words. See `cv-translate-de`'s rules on rebuilding rather
than translating for the same principle applied to resumes.

## Reading the signal from the posting

Before choosing a register, look at:

- **Industry and company type** — see the table below.
- **The language the posting itself uses.** A posting full of "wir" and
  casual phrasing signals a different register than one written in dense
  formal Sie-form corporate language.
- **"Du" vs "Sie" in the posting.** Only mirror "Du" if the posting itself
  uses it throughout. Never initiate informality.
- **Careers-page tone**, if the user has shared it — a startup careers page
  full of team photos and casual copy licenses a more direct letter than a
  Mittelstand firm's plain corporate site.

## Tone by company type

| Company type | Register | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Startup | Direct, conversational (either language) | Confident first-person claims, contractions fine in English |
| Corporate / enterprise | Professional, measured | Matches the posting's own vocabulary, no over-familiarity |
| Public sector, university | Formal, criteria-driven | Explicit point-by-point match to stated selection criteria |
| Research lab | Precise, evidence-first | Names the paper, method, or open problem directly |
| Agency, creative | Personality forward | More voice, more risk-tolerant hooks acceptable |
| German Mittelstand | Formal, sincere, Sie-form | Sachlich as described above; no imported directness |

## Don't overcorrect into stiffness

Sachlich is not the same as robotic. A German letter can still contain one
honest, specific line about motivation — it just has to be a fact
("Ihre Entscheidung, [konkrete Firmenentscheidung], deckt sich mit
[konkrete eigene Erfahrung]"), not an assertion of enthusiasm
("Ich bin sehr motiviert und würde mich riesig freuen"). The fact carries
the motivation; the adjective doesn't need to.
