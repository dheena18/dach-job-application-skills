# Handling gaps and obvious questions

## Only address what the posting actually made a hard requirement

Don't manufacture a confession for something the posting never asked
about. If a gap, career switch, or missing skill isn't a named requirement
or an obvious mismatch a reader would notice, leave it out — bringing it up
unprompted just plants a doubt that wasn't there.

When it is a named hard requirement the candidate doesn't fully meet, this
skill follows the same policy `resume-tailor` uses: **ask the user before
deciding** whether and how to address it. Don't decide unilaterally.

## The pattern, once addressing one is the right call

1. **Name it briefly** — roughly 5% of the letter's words, not a paragraph.
2. **State it as fact, not apology.** No "unfortunately," no "I'm sorry to
   say," no hedging language that invites more doubt than the fact itself
   does.
3. **Pivot immediately to readiness or a mitigating fact.** The gap gets
   one sentence; the pivot gets at least as much space.

This usually lands in Absatz 3 / the "Fit" paragraph, not the close.

## Examples (generic patterns — replace with the real, honest specifics)

- **Employment gap:** "I took [period] off for [reason]. I kept current
  with the field through [concrete thing] and I'm fully available from
  [date]."
- **Career switch:** "I spent [N] years in [prior field] before moving into
  [new field]. That background is why I understand [specific relevant
  aspect] your product depends on."
- **Overqualified:** "I'm deliberately looking for a hands-on role after
  [N] years managing. I miss building."
- **Relocation:** "I'm based in [city] and can relocate to [target city]
  within [timeframe], at my own cost."
- **Language level below what's asked:** "My German is [honest CEFR level]
  and I use it daily at work; I'm taking [level] classes this term." Never
  claim a CEFR level that isn't accurate — a German-language interview
  tests it in the first minute.
- **One missing skill, not the whole role:** "I haven't run [X] in
  production. I've deployed on [related tool], and ramping up on [X] would
  be the first thing I'd do."

## What "state as fact" rules out

Before: "Unfortunately, I don't have direct experience with Kubernetes,
but I'm a fast learner and I'm sure I could pick it up quickly."

After: "I haven't run Kubernetes in production. I've deployed on ECS and
Docker Swarm, and ramping up on k8s would be the first thing I'd do."

The second version states the same fact in fewer words, without an
apology, a self-assessment claim ("fast learner"), or a hedge ("I'm sure I
could"). See `banned-phrases-de-en.md` for the broader list this draws from.
