# The German Kurzprofil

Sources: karrierebibel.de, lebenslauf.de, karriere.at, lebenslaufapp.ch
(Swiss), lebenslauf-easy.de, onlinelebenslauf.com. Cross-checked for
convergence; single-source specifics are marked as such.

## What it's called, and whether it's expected at all

Called "Kurzprofil", "Profil", "Persönliches/Berufliches Profil", or
colloquially "Über mich" — terminology isn't standardized, use "Kurzprofil"
as the section heading unless the person has a reason to prefer another.
It sits directly under the contact header, before Berufserfahrung — the
first content block a reader sees.

It is explicitly optional, not a fixed required Lebenslauf component the
way Berufserfahrung or Ausbildung are. Documented recommendation: include
it specifically for experienced professionals, executives, and project
leads, who have a track record worth condensing into one place. Skip it for
someone early-career with little to summarize yet — an empty-feeling
Kurzprofil is worse than no Kurzprofil.

## The core difference from an American resume summary

This is the point most likely to go wrong in a straight translation.
Documented cross-source finding: German professional writing in this
section is explicitly more sachlich (factual, reserved) than the more
enthusiastic, narrative American convention. Multiple independent sources
warn against the same underlying pattern from different angles:

- Overly personal or subjective phrasing risks being read as unprofessional
  or inviting negative snap judgments ("Fehleinschätzungen und
  Abwertungen").
- Sehr persönliche Formulierungen, irony, or jokes "wirken fehl am Platz"
  (read as out of place).
- Negative language or self-doubt "hat keinen Platz."
- One Swiss source names it most directly: "No excessive self-reflection —
  focus on achievements with data/quantifiable results rather than personal
  feelings," calling clichéd/soft phrasing "Gift" (poison).

Grammatical first person is not the issue — karrierebibel.de states the
Kurzprofil is written "ausschließlich in Ich-Form" (exclusively in first
person). What's flagged is reflective, feelings-oriented, or aspirational
musing about what the person personally values or wants — the content
category, not the grammatical person.

## Structure

Consensus shape: **role/title + years of experience + domain/specialization
→ core competencies → one concrete proof point with a number → optionally,
a fit/goal line tailored to the target.**

Register: full short sentences forming a brief flowing paragraph
(Fließtext) is the majority convention — not the bullet-fragment style used
in the rest of a German Lebenslauf. Knackige Hauptsätze (short, punchy main
clauses), not Schachtelsätze (nested subordinate-clause chains). Active
voice. No unevidenced claims of "teamfähig", "belastbar", "motiviert" — the
same anti-buzzword principle as the English convention, with German-specific
examples.

## Length

Strong convergence: **50-100 words, 3-6 sentences, roughly 4-6 lines.** One
Swiss source is slightly more generous (up to 200 words); one German source
suggests as little as 2-3 lines. Treat 50-100 words as the documented norm
and default to the tighter end of it.

## Worked examples from the sources

- Proof-point model (karrierebibel.de): "...23 % Umsatzsteigerung in einem
  Jahr."
- Full example (lebenslauf-easy.de, experienced candidate): "Als Marketing
  Manager habe ich mich in den letzten 10 Jahren auf den Bereich Social
  Media spezialisiert. Neben der Leitung eines Teams von sieben Mitarbeitern
  habe ich in meiner aktuellen Position die Online-Präsenz unseres
  Unternehmens erhöht."
- Good/bad contrast (karriere.at): weak = generic trait listing; strong =
  "Fachinformatiker mit 5 Jahren Erfahrung in Netzwerksicherheit...
  Verantwortlich für die Einführung von Microsoft Azure... für 250
  Mitarbeitende."

All three follow the same shape: role + years/specialization + a number
that couldn't be guessed from the job title alone.
